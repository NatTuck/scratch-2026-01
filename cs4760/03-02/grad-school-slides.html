Master’s Degree Pathways (Deep Thoughts With Interest Rates)

1. Same Major

  • CS → MS CS
  • IT → MS IT
  • Robotics → MS Robotics

Why: More depth in what you already know

Master’s: Related Fields (Pivot Harder: The Sequel)

2. Pivot to Adjacent Tech

  • CS → Data Science / AI-ML
  • CS → Cybersecurity
  • IT → Software Engineering
  • Any → Computer Engineering

Why: Specialization = higher pay, more job security

Master’s: Different Field (The Career Reset Button Press Carefully)

3. Complete Change

  • CS → MBA (tech management track)
  • CS → Healthcare Informatics
  • CS → Law (IP / tech law)
  • Any → Teaching (MAT)

Why: Broaden your career options

Dual Degrees (Twice the Tuition Half the Sleep)

  • MS CS + Robotics

Why: Business + Technical = Chief Technology Officer

Why Consider Grad School? (Let’s Pretend This Is Mandatory)

1. Higher Starting Salary

  • Bachelor’s CS: ~$76K average (2025)
  • Master’s CS: ~$94K average (Class of 2026)
  • PhD (industry): $180K+ starting

That’s a meaningful difference

Why Consider Grad School? (Follow the Money)

2. Access to Specializations

Roles that basically require grad school:

  • Machine Learning Engineer
  • Computer Vision Researcher
  • Robotics Engineer (some positions)
  • Research Scientist

You can get these with a BS, but it takes years of self-study

Why Consider Grad School?

3. Career Pivoting

“I want to do AI but my BS was in IT”

A master’s is the cleanest way to switch tracks

Reality: Many employers screen by degree

Why Consider Grad School? (Just Pivot: The Documentary)

4. Job Security and Advancement

  • More degrees = more senior positions
  • Management tracks often prefer advanced degrees
  • Layoffs hit junior devs harder

Why Consider Grad School? (They Can’t Fire Me If I’m Still In School)

5. Faculty / Research Career

If you want to be a professor

  • PhD is required (non-negotiable)
  • Plan for 5-7 years of low pay
  • Published research is currency

Honest take: Academic job market is brutal

Why NOT Grad School? (You Could Be Making Money RIGHT NOW)

2. Industry Experience Wins

Fact: A dev with 5 years of experience > fresh MS grad

  • You learn things in industry you can’t in school
  • Companies value proven production code
  • Senior devs with BS out-earn fresh MS

Why NOT Grad School? (XP Matters More Than Your GPA)

3. Many Tech Jobs Don’t Need It

Roles that are totally accessible with BS

  • Frontend Developer
  • Backend Developer
  • Full-Stack Engineer
  • DevOps / SRE
  • Most “Software Engineer” roles at big companies

Why NOT Grad School? (Bachelor’s Degree Still Valid Barely)

4. The “I’ll figure out what to do in grad school” Trap

Bad reason to go

  • “I don’t know what else to do”
  • “My parents want me to”
  • “It seems like the next step”

This is a $100K+ mistake

Why NOT Grad School? (The I’ll Figure It Out Pipeline)

5. You Can Always Go Back

Part-time options exist

  • Online programs (Georgia Tech OMSCS: $7K total)
  • Executive MBAs
  • Company-sponsored degrees

No penalty for waiting

Why NOT Grad School? (It’s Called Burnout Because That’s What You’ll Be)

6. Academic Burnout is Real

Grad school is

  • 5-7 years of poverty ($30-50K/year)
  • Uncertainty about advisor fit
  • Publication pressure
  • Imposter syndrome

Ask yourself: Do you need this, or do you just think you do?

The Real Talk: When Grad School Makes Sense (In Certain Extreme Circumstances)

1. You want a very specific, specialized role

  • ML Engineer at top labs
  • Research Scientist
  • Faculty position
  • Highly specialized robotics

2. Career pivot from non-CS field

  • Biology → Computational Biology
  • Liberal Arts → Data Science (with bridge program)

3. Company requires it

  • Some research positions
  • Government clearances

The Real Talk: When to Skip It (How to Win Friends and Save Money)

1. You just want “more education”

That’s what books are for. Free ones.

2. You think it guarantees a job

It doesn’t. Experience > degree, usually.

3. You’re avoiding the job market

The job market will still be there in 2 years

Practical Realities: Applications (Apply Directly to the Forehead)

What you need

  • GPA: Usually 3.0 minimum (3.5+ competitive)
  • Statement of Purpose: Why this program? Why now?
  • Letters of Recommendation: Need 2-3, from people who know your work
  • GRE: Many schools dropped this (check!)

Making Your Decision (Decisions Were Made)

Questions to ask yourself

  1. What job do I want? Does it require grad school?
  2. Can I get there another way?
  3. Am I passionate about this topic?
  4. Can I afford it (financially and mentally)?
  5. What will I regret not doing?
